BAB I
Introduction
1.1 Background
The video game industry has evolved into one of the largest entertainment sectors in the world, generating hundreds of billions of dollars in revenue and reaching more than three billion players, surpassing the combined earnings of the film and music industries (Edward Goh et al., 2023). This rapid growth is driven by the expansion of mobile gaming, the dominance of the Asia-Pacific region as the largest revenue contributor, which together position games as a highly influential global cultural medium. Within this strategic position, cultural representation in video games has become a crucial issue because games function not only as entertainment but also as “cultural ambassadors” that shape perceptions, introduce traditions, and influence players' understanding of both local and global identities (Mario cerezo-pizzaro et al., 2023). Various studies indicate that inclusive and diverse representation can enhance players' sense of connection, empathy, and appreciation of differences, while stereotypical portrayals risk reproducing biases and misconceptions about particular groups or cultures. Therefore, examining the game industry and cultural representation in video games is essential to understand how character design, narrative, and game worlds can contribute to diversity, intercultural dialogue, and the reduction of stereotypes in the era of digital globalization (Erin C Cassese et al., 2023).
The global video game industry draws inspiration from diverse mythologies and cultural narratives, yet representation remains heavily toward Western traditions such as Norse and Greek mythology, as well as East Asian cultures like China, Japan, and Korea (Rahman, 2024; Mohd Fairuz & Perng Jeu, 2018). In contrast, Malay-inspired cultures and characters receive significantly less exposure, even though Southeast Asian gamers constitute a large and growing player base worldwide. Malay cultural heritage itself is rich with folklore, myths, and historical narratives that have been passed down across generations in oral, written, and symbolic forms, functioning not only as entertainment but also as vehicles for moral values, philosophical worldviews, and a shared sense of collective identity within the Malay community (Husain et al., 2023). (Nasir & Insyirah, 2019). Legendary figures such as Raja Ali Haji, Hang Tuah, Puteri Gunung Ledang, and mythical creatures like the Naga and Orang Bunian represent bravery, wisdom, and a harmonious relationship with nature, illustrating the narrative depth and symbolic potential embedded in Malay traditions. This cultural wealth offers substantial, yet still underutilized, potential as a source of inspiration for narratives, characters, and world-building in contemporary video games and other digital media.
While this under-representation of Malay-inspired characters and Southeast Asian cultural elements in the global video game market presents an opportunity for Southeast Asian game developers to thrive in, compared with the rich depictions of other well-known mythologies, authentic and nuanced representations of Malay culture remain scarce: the complexity and originality of Malay folklore have yet to be fully explored or appreciated in mainstream video games (Rahman, 2024; Mohd Fairuz & Perng Jeu, 2018; Mohd Yusof et al., 2024). Several factors contribute to this under-representation, including the industry's focus on commercially profitable products, lack of cultural diversity within game development teams, stereotypical portrayals of non-Western cultures, and limited global awareness or promotion of Malay cultural heritage (Husain et al., 2023). While these are real challenges faced by the global video game industry in embracing cultural diversity fully, these challenges can also be viewed as fertile grounds that can be capitalized to promote culture and fulfill a demand for Southeast Asian and Malay representation among gamers (Subri et al., 2024).

BAB II
Literature Review


2.1 Prior Studies
2.1.1 Malay Culture in Pop Culture
The archipelagic region of Maritime Southeast Asia is deeply embedded in a wealth of folklore, myths, and historical narratives that continue to shape its cultural identity. While these traditional stories have remained as oral, written, and symbolic forms, their relevance extends beyond mere entertainment (Husain et al., 2023). They are deeply ingrained in the fabric of contemporary pop culture, influencing a variety of media, including film, music, and online content primarily prevalent in platforms such as YouTube and TikTok (Mario cerezo-pizzaro et al., 2023), where they remain vital sources of inspiration and cultural expression (Fatin Nadia Asmadi and Rafuzan Jafar, 2023).
Malay folklore has found a prominent place in the narratives of film, music, and digital media, where figures like Hang Tuah, Raja Ali Haji, and Puteri Gunung Ledang continue to inspire audiences. These stories explore universal values of bravery, loyalty, wisdom, self-sacrifice, and the harmonious relationship between men and nature (Rozita Che Rodi, 2021; Yusdani, 2024). Through contemporary renditions, these mythic characters and supernatural beings, such as the Naga (serpent dragon) and Orang Bunian (elves and druids), appear in modern media, reflecting a world where balance and respect for nature are key thematic elements (Hendrokumoro et al., 2025; Junaini et al., 2025; Sirait et al., 2024).
However, despite their presence in various forms of media, Malay folklore has been underrepresented in the video game industry. While cultural motifs and mythologies have permeated pop culture through mainstream media, they have yet to achieve the same level of engagement in video games. For game developers looking to create culturally rich and distinctive content, Malay folklore presents an untapped wellspring of narrative depth, rich symbolism, and world-building potential (Mohd Yusof et al., 2024; Yaacob, 2024; Nizam, 2024; Ramli, 2024). This gap presents a significant opportunity for innovation, as the incorporation of  Malay cultural elements into video games could bring a fresh and diverse perspective to the global gaming landscape.

2.1.2. The Global Video Game Industry: Mythologies and Cultural Representation
Despite the appeal of myth-inspired superhero figures and legendary narratives in global pop culture, media representation disproportionately privileges Western mythologies namely Norse and Greek and select East Asian traditions, often sidelining Southeast Asian motifs. The prevalence of Western-centric characters and mythological elements in mainstream video games has resulted in a critical under-representation of Malay-inspired stories and cultural elements. This is evident even as Southeast Asia emerges as a major market, with its player base numbering in the hundreds of millions annually, though precise statistics need to be referenced for accuracy (Rahman, 2024; Mohd Fairuz & Perng Jeu, 2018).
Scholars of game studies and cultural representation note that games frequently rely on the “familiar”: mythic figures from Europe or East Asia that have already proven market‑viable. Meanwhile, Southeast Asian cultural content often appears marginally or through superficial “exotic” trappings rather than full narrative integration. For example, a thematic study of video games and cultural heritage found that while games have direct cultural transmission impacts, much of the focus has been on dominant regions and there is a gap in representing less‑served cultural zones (Mario Cerezo Pizzaro 2023).
Game studios often prefer mythologies that have already been validated by mass-market success. Western mythologies, in particular, come with built-in recognition and fewer localization risks (Xiangyun Zhang et al., 2025). Many large studios are based in North America, Europe, or Japan, and their teams may lack deep familiarity with Southeast Asian cultural contexts to properly and respectfully adapt it into a video game. When local culture is used, there is a risk of superficial or stereotyped portrayals. A report on Asian representation in games underscores this risk of appropriation or shallow reference. (Gusanti, 2025)
Outside the region, many developers and audiences lack awareness of the richness of Malay mythologies. This cultural-visibility gap leads to fewer references, fewer pitched game projects, and less cross-regional collaboration (Rahman & Sidek, 2024). While indie and regional studios do exist, they often have fewer resources for global marketing and face distribution barriers. As a result, many Malay-inspired games remain niche or regionally confined, failing to impact mainstream game culture globally (Wong, 2024). Furthermore, game industry narratives tend to recycle common tropes such as the hero’s journey, war-fantasy, or dystopia, rather than exploring culturally specific worlds. Creating a game that is deeply anchored in Malay myth requires investment in new narrative design, mythic research, and world-building efforts that some studios may hesitate to undertake (Vandewalle, 2023).
This lack of representation should not simply be seen as a challenge but as a promising opportunity for local game creators. Authentic, nuanced, and culturally-rooted depictions of Malay heritage and culture have substantial potential to meet the ever-growing users’ demands for distinct, culturally diverse content in video games, even if this demand remains niche within the global context (Darvishinia & Goodson, 2024). By drawing on the originality and complexity of Malay folklore, developers can both celebrate local identities and contribute to the diversification of narratives and culture representation within the gaming industry, thereby fostering greater cultural appreciation and visibility on the world stage. (Mohd Yusof et al., 2024; Rahman & Sidek, 2024; Wong, 2024)


2.2 Research Gap
Current academic discourse indicates that despite the inherent richness of Malay mythology, it remains significantly marginalized within the mainstream video game industry, overshadowed by the ubiquity of Norse, Greek, and East Asian cultural narratives. Previous studies highlight that even when Southeast Asian archetypes appear, they are frequently relegated to peripheral roles, lacking narrative centrality or robust world-building counterparts. Addressing this documented gap, this research proposes a systematic model for integrating these complex narratives by presenting an ARPG prototype developed under the GDLC framework, thereby offering a practical methodology for the authentic representation and global promotion of Malay heritage in interactive media.

Metodologi Penelitian
3.1 Research Flow
  

This research contributes to the body of knowledge on cultural preservation through digital media specific to Malay heritage and folklore, particularly in the gaming industry. To design and develop an ARPG prototype that integrates Malay cultural narratives and aesthetics into video game applications, showcasing a model for cultural preservation and revitalization of Malay heritage through the media of video games.
This research begins with literature review thus reveals a critical disconnect between the documented depth of Malay mythologies and their relatively minimal presence in mainstream video games. While Western traditions especially Greek and Norse and East Asian cultures are widely incorporated into commercial titles and have been extensively discussed in both industry and academic discourse, Southeast Asian and particularly Malay cultural narratives remain underexplored and, when present, are often treated superficially. Addressing this under‑representation requires not only more inclusive and culturally grounded content but also concrete design frameworks and prototypes that demonstrate how Malay folklore can be translated into compelling ARPG mechanics, narratives, and worlds, which this study seeks to provide.
The next step process is the Game Development Life Cycle (GDLC) phase, which will involve the creation of an Action Role-Playing Game (ARPG) prototype. This prototype will be designed to incorporate key elements of Malay cultural narratives, aesthetics, and mythological systems into the game's storyline, character development, and environmental design. By utilizing these elements, the game will serve not only as a tool for entertainment but also as a vehicle for cultural education and preservation.

3.2 GDLC Framework
This study aims to develop an Action RPG game inspired by Malay culture and folklore. Blender is used to create 3D assets, and the Unity Engine functions as the tool to assemble those assets into a complete game. The development process follows the Game Development Life Cycle (GDLC) method, which includes the stages of: initiation, pre-production, production, and testing. If deficiencies are found during the testing stage, the process returns to the pre-production stage. However, if no errors are detected, the development continues into early-acess and concludes with the release stage.  
3.2.1 Initiation
The initiation phase marks the beginning of the development of the Action Role-Playing Game (ARPG) based on Malay culture. During this phase, the project will be formally launched, and the development team will be assembled. Key objectives, such as defining the vision of the game, establishing the core concept, and setting the overall scope of the project, will be outlined. This phase will also involve conducting research on Malay folklore, history, and traditions to ensure the game's content is authentic and culturally respectful. Initial planning will focus on identifying the resources, tools, and team members needed for the project’s success. The groundwork for game design, including narrative themes, character creation, and world-building inspired by Malay heritage, will also begin during this phase, setting the foundation for a culturally immersive and engaging ARPG experience.
________________
3.2.2 Pre-Production
In the pre-production phase, the concept for the Action Role-Playing Game (ARPG) based on Malay culture will be developed. This includes defining the core gameplay mechanics, such as combat systems, character progression, and exploration, all while integrating elements of Malay folklore, mythology, and cultural aesthetics. The narrative will be built around traditional stories, legendary heroes, and mythic beings like the Raja Ali Haji, offering players an immersive experience in a richly detailed world inspired by Malay heritage. Visual designs will reflect the unique aesthetics of Malay culture, from architecture and landscapes to character designs and costumes. During this phase, the development team will also outline the game’s technical requirements, gameplay features, and key milestones, ensuring that the game’s concept is both engaging and feasible for the production process ahead. Additionally, a comprehensive Game Design Document (GDD) will be created. This document will serve as the blueprint for the game, detailing every aspect of the gameplay, art, narrative, and technical specifications. The GDD will outline the design of levels, characters, gameplay mechanics, art style, user interface, and sound, ensuring a cohesive vision across all departments. It will also include milestones, schedules, and resource planning, making it an essential tool for tracking progress and guiding the development process from pre-production to post-launch.
3.2.3 Production
In the production phase, the development of the Action Role-Playing Game (ARPG) based on Malay culture will move into full-scale creation. This phase begins with the development of 3D assets, including the design of characters, enemies, environments, and objects that reflect the unique visual style and aesthetic of Malay culture. 3D modeling tools such as, but not limited to Blender and Photoshop will be used in the process of assets creation. The world and environment will be meticulously crafted to immerse players in settings inspired by traditional Malay landscapes, folklore, and architecture. Alongside this, the game logic will be programmed using the Unity Engine software suite. Characters and enemies will be designed based on mythological and legendary figures from Malay heritage, with careful attention to their roles, abilities, and interactions within the game. A combat system inspired by the rogue-lite genre will be programmed to offer dynamic, action-packed gameplay, blending traditional mechanics with culturally inspired elements. The narrative will be integrated into the game through dialogue and cutscenes that bring the story to life, drawing from the rich myths and legends of the Malay world. Finally, audio elements such as music, sound effects, and voice acting will be created to enhance the immersive experience, using traditional instruments and language to further connect players with the cultural themes of the game.


1. Narrative Development 
The narrative structure for each episode will be designed to follow established arcs, segmented into three key parts:


Start: The story begins by establishing the protagonist as a dedicated scholar living within a flourishing Malay Sultanate. Initially, the focus is on his intellectual pursuit, as he travels to various regions to gather inspiration to complete his work.
Conflict: During his travels, the protagonist discovers a magical pen artifact, which coincides with the sudden invasion by hostile forces. The conflict centers on the scholar's transformation into a defender; he must utilize the magical pen to manifest the contents of his books into physical form to fight off the invaders.
Resolution: The narrative concludes with the successful repulsion of the invading army. The resolution highlights the triumph of the scholar, symbolizing that knowledge and power go hand-in-hand in protecting the sovereignty of the land.


2.  Game Asset Creation
In the production phase of the GDLC, all visual assets will be created in Blender, Photoshop, and Aseprite to maintain a unified art style. This phase will focus on the development of all necessary graphical elements, including characters, environments, objects, and other visual components essential to the game aesthetic and gameplay. The process will involve the creation of detailed 3D models, textures, and animations, ensuring that each asset is meticulously designed to meet the technical and artistic requirements of the game, character 3D models, 2D Sprites, Background art / environment design, User Interface (UI) elements, Music & sound design.
3. Game Programming
Using the Unity Engine as the primary development platform, with Visual Studio as the scripting editor, unique mechanics will be implemented for each episode. The core programming workflow involves:

* Developing a base code architecture in C# to manage player movement, inventory systems, level streaming, and the core application flow.
* Scripting dynamic combat mechanics, including attack, dodging, and special abilities. Artificial Intelligence (AI) scripts will be developed for enemies dictating their behavior patterns, pathfinding, and attack states.
* Implementing a dialogue and quest system that triggers cutscenes and advances the storyline based on player interaction with NPCs and environmental objects.


This proposed narrative structure will be delivered through the main story, character interactions, and wave completion. The specific content is developed using an applied research approach, synthesizing authentic historical records and folklore with creative elements. This integration ensures the narrative is not based on generalized assumptions, but is instead a culturally grounded reimagining that respects the source material while enhancing the gameplay experience.
3.2.4 Testing
In the private testing phase, the game will first be evaluated internally by the development team using White Box and Black Box Testing to ensure thorough assessment of the game's code and internal mechanics. Developers will conduct systematic playtesting to identify bugs, performance issues, and design inconsistencies, while also evaluating gameplay balance, user interface clarity, and overall user experience. To ensure working software, White Box Testing will allow testers to access game algorithms and variables, enabling them to evaluate the expected game behavior and compare it to the software. Bug identification will be enhanced by reviewing the game’s source code to detect logical flaws, unhandled exceptions, and edge cases that could lead to crashes or unexpected behavior. Performance issues will be analyzed by examining the game’s code and optimizing critical sections that affect frame rates and load times, as well as stress testing to observe how the system behaves under heavy load. User interface clarity will be evaluated through code-level inspection of UI elements to ensure they are appropriately mapped to user actions, ensuring responsiveness and ease of navigation. Finally, overall user experience will be assessed by reviewing player interaction data captured during playtesting, identifying areas of improvement in narrative flow, and analyzing any technical obstacles that might disrupt gameplay. To measure gameplay difficulty, Black Box Testing is used to analyze the difficulty curve by comparing player performance metrics measured through game progress and wave completion, then adjusting difficulty variables between test sessions. Feedback from this internal testing will be documented and used to iteratively refine game mechanics, narrative flow, technical stability, and game enjoyment before the project proceeds to public testing in the early access phase.
3.2.5 Early Access
The early access phase of the game will involve public testing through platforms such as Steamworks Early Access and/or itch.io. These platforms will allow players to engage with the game before its official release, providing valuable feedback that can be used to refine and improve the game. Additionally, social media promotions will be leveraged to create awareness and attract players to participate in the early access phase. This approach will not only help in gathering insights from the gaming community but also build a loyal following ahead of the full launch.
